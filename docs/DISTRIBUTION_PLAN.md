# Distribution and adoption plan

## Priority sequence

### Tier 1 — educator adoption and durable discovery

1. **CSTA community and relevant chapter channels:** lead with a ready 15-minute lesson, not the repository.
2. **OER Commons:** create a resource record with grade band, subject, language, license, accessibility boundary, and teacher guide.
3. **MERLOT:** submit the suite as a learning material and invite structured peer review.
4. **ACM SIGCSE community channels:** emphasize the architecture, classroom-use pattern, and open test evidence; avoid a generic promotional post.
5. **AP Computer Science teacher communities:** share one curriculum-mapped activity and a direct classroom-use note, subject to each community’s promotion rules.
6. **GitHub Education/community channels:** lead with the offline/single-file architecture, contribution guide, and educator issue template.

### Tier 2 — technical attention and repository growth

1. Show HN.
2. A technical article on DEV Community or Hashnode explaining the one-file architecture and verification design.
3. A carefully scoped submission to an active “awesome” list for computer-science education, AI education, or algorithm visualization after checking its contribution rules.
4. Targeted Reddit communities only where self-promotion is permitted; share a substantive lesson or engineering write-up rather than dropping a bare link.
5. Bluesky and Mastodon threads with one applet per post and a final repository link.
6. Lobsters only if an account and an appropriate invitation/channel are already available; use a technical framing rather than a launch announcement.

### Tier 3 — research and archival credibility

1. Archive the tagged release and evidence bundle in a DOI-granting repository.
2. Publish the corrected Paper 7 design/tool account with the exact artifact version and commit.
3. Evaluate fit for the Journal of Open Source Education or a computing-education demo/workshop venue.
4. Deposit reusable lesson materials in OER repositories separately from the software archive so educators can discover them by learning objective.

## Lower-priority channels

Product Hunt and Indie Hackers may generate general product traffic but are less aligned with educator adoption. Use them only after Tier 1 records are complete and the launch page converts visitors into a first applet interaction.

## Campaign codes

Use only the documented allow-list, for example `?ap_src=linkedin`, `?ap_src=csta`, or `?ap_src=oercommons`. Use the direct URL for communities that discourage tracking parameters. Never create person-specific or classroom-specific codes.

## Four-week cadence

- **Week 0:** archive, release notes, signed-out smoke test, educator feedback template.
- **Week 1:** LinkedIn educator post, CSTA/OER submissions, direct outreach to a small number of teachers.
- **Week 2:** Show HN and technical article; respond to issues and document recurring questions.
- **Week 3:** applet-specific posts and curriculum examples; submit appropriate awesome-list PRs.
- **Week 4:** publish an aggregate uptake note using the reporting protocol; do not report raw or low-count data.
